# Introduction

Since 1882, the Glenmont Estate, pictured above, has been one of the premier estates of Llewellyn Park, a private community in West Orange, New Jersey. In 1886, the Glenmont became the home of Thomas and Mina Edison. For the next six decades, domestic workers, including cooks, chambermaids, parlor maids, laundresses, housemen, gardeners, and general servants, served as support staff to Mina Edison in the maintenance of the home. ​

Glenmont’s maintenance followed rhythms of production, restoration, and care. Over the years, the Glenmont staff adapted their labor to shifting technologies, the Edisons’ seasonal migrations to Florida, changes in family structure, and evolving expectations of elite domestic life in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. Some employees remained for decades, and others appeared only briefly in census lines or voucher books. Collectively, their work supported the upkeep of the estate. Meals were prepared, fires were laid, linens were renewed, guests were received, children were tended, and the grounds were maintained by the effort of people with their own stories. Tracing Glenmont’s history from 1885 to 1947 means tracing the evolving, often invisible labor of the people who made it a historic property.
